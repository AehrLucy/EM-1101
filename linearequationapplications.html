Why This Matters for Calculus

Mastering word problems and linear modeling is a vital prerequisite for Calculus. In higher-level mathematics, you will constantly translate real-world scenarios into mathematical functions. These exact algebraic translation skills form the bedrock for setting up optimization constraints (finding maximum/minimum values), related rates models (calculating how rates change with respect to time, like planes meeting or pumps filling pools), and crafting linear approximations.

📖 Mini-Lecture: Step-by-Step Problem Solving Guide

Step 1

Understand & Identify Variables

Read the problem carefully. Identify what is unknown and assign clear variable names (e.g., let w = width).

Step 2

Translate Words to Equations

Convert descriptive sentences into algebraic equations using known formulas (perimeter, rates, mixtures, motion).

Step 3

Solve the Equation

Isolate the variable step-by-step using fundamental algebraic properties (addition/multiplication principles).

Step 4

Check & Interpret in Context

Verify if your numerical answer makes physical sense (e.g., can time or distance be negative? Do units match?).

Common Keyword Translation Reference

• "More than", "Sum", "Increased by"
→ Addition (+)
• "Less than", "Difference", "Decreased"
→ Subtraction (-)
• "Per", "At a rate of", "Product"
→ Multiplication / Division

✍️ Practice Exercises (12 Problems)

Geometry / Perimeter

1. If the perimeter of a rectangle is 68 meters and the length is 14 meters more than the width, what are dimensions of the rectangle?

Number Relations

2. The sum of two numbers is -4 and their difference is 6. What are the numbers?

Work Rate

3. If one pump can fill a pool in 16 hours and if two pumps can fill the pool in 6 hours, how fast can the second pump fill the pool?

Financial / Markup

4. A widget is being sold in a store for $135.40 and has been marked up 7%. How much did the store pay for the widget?

Financial / Discount

5. A store is having a 30% off sale and one item is now being sold for $9.95. What was the original price of the item?

Uniform Motion

6. Two planes start out 2800 km apart and move towards each other meeting after 3.5 hours. One plane flies at 75 km/hour slower than the other plane. What was the speed of each plane?

Uniform Motion

7. Mike starts out 35 feet in front of Kim and they both start moving towards the right at the same time. Mike moves at 2 ft/sec while Kim moves at 3.4 ft/sec. How long will it take for Kim to catch up with Mike?

Work Rate

8. A pump can empty a pool in 7 hours and a different pump can empty the same pool in 12 hours. How long does it take for both pumps working together to empty the pool?

Work Rate

9. John can paint a house in 28 hours. John and Dave can paint the house in 17 hours working together. How long would it take Dave to paint the house by himself?

Mixture Solution

10. How much of a 20% acid solution should we add to 20 gallons of a 42% acid solution to get a 35% acid solution?

Mixture Solution

11. We need 100 liters of a 25% saline solution and we only have a 14% solution and a 60% solution. How much of each should we mix together to get the 100 liters of the 25% solution?

Geometry / Fencing

12. We want to fence in a field whose length is twice the width and we have 80 feet of fencing material. If we use all the fencing material what would the dimensions of the field be?
