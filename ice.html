ME 3115 Midterm: Historical and Experimental Engine Forensics Lab

Internal Combustion Engines

1. Overview and Objective

In place of the scheduled midterm, you will analyze one historical or experimental four-stroke, spark-ignition reciprocating engine using the virtual engine bench at realenginesimulator.com. You will (1) set up and document the baseline, (2) compute geometry, kinematics and performance by hand, (3) run a constrained tuning study aimed at a bottleneck of the engine’s layout, and (4) use the engine’s own P-V indicator diagram to reconcile the ideal air-standard cycle with real loss mechanisms.

Several students share each engine, but your numbers will differ: your checkpoints, crank angle, tuning track, design objective, first step and hot-gas k are generated from your Student ID (Section 4). Matching numbers between two submissions trigger an integrity review. Estimated effort: 10 to 14 hours.

2. Scope and Formulas (Course Notation)

Every engine in the pool is a four-stroke SI piston engine, so the same formulas apply to all of them. Use the notation and constants of the course Formula Reference: bore b, stroke s, cylinders nc, nR = 2, specific-heat ratio k. Cold air-standard constants: R = 0.287 kJ/kg·K, cp = 1.005, cv = 0.718 kJ/kg·K, k = 1.4.

TopicFormulas used in this lab
GeometryVd = (π/4) b² s  |  Vd,total = nc·Vd  |  r = (Vc+Vd)/Vc = 1 + Vd/Vc, so Vc = Vd/(r−1)  |  ε = a/l = s/2l
KinematicsMean piston speed: Úp = 2Ns [m/s] (N in rev/s)
V(θ) = Vc[1 + ((r−1)/2)((1−cosθ) + (1/ε)(1 − √(1−ε²sin²θ)))]
Up(θ) = (π/2)Úp sinθ [1 + εcosθ/√(1−ε²sin²θ)]
Power & MEPBrake power: &Wdot;b = 2πτN  |  bmep = nR&Wdot;b/(Vd,total·N) = 4πτ/Vd,total
Indicated power: &Wdot;i = nc·Wi·(N/nR), with Wi = imep·Vd
ηm = &Wdot;b/&Wdot;i = bmep/imep  |  fmep = imep − bmep
Fuel & efficiencybsfc = ṁf/&Wdot;b  |  ηb = &Wdot;b/(ṁfqc) = 1/(bsfc·qc)  |  ηb = 3600/(bsfc[kg/kW·h] · qc[kJ/kg])
Breathingev = ṁin/(ρiVd,total(N/2)), ρi = Pi/(RTi) (in the simulator ev is an input slider; use it to check the air-fuel ratio)
CyclesηOtto = 1 − 1/rk−1  |  isentropic: T2/T1 = rk−1, P2/P1 = rk
First law & polytropicQ1–2 − W1–2 = mcv(T2−T1)  |  P2/P1 = (V1/V2)n, T2/T1 = (V1/V2)n−1  |  W1–2 = (P1V1 − P2V2)/(n−1)

What differs between engines: forced induction raises bmep (formula unchanged; ηOtto still uses the geometric r); sleeve valves (Sabre, Centaurus) are modelled with poppet valves in the simulator, so declare it; fuel heating value qc is 43.5 MJ/kg for aviation gasoline engines and 44.0 MJ/kg for pump-gasoline engines, unless the simulator states otherwise. Diesel, Dual and Brayton cycles from the course are not used here because no engine in the pool is a diesel or two-stroke.

5. Mapping Your Engine onto the Simulator Presets

The free simulator has no custom engine builder. You work with a preset and edit its Specs controls (Rows, Cylinders per row, Bore, Stroke, Compression ratio, Supercharger, Fuel grade, Tuning). Follow these rules.

  1. Choose the preset. Radial engines use the radial preset (for example the P&W R-2800 Double Wasp). Engines with two rows of 7 or 9 cylinders fit its sliders directly (R-3350, Centaurus, BMW 801). For X, H, W, U, square-four and 4-row engines, choose the nearest preset in the Engine list that lets you match bore, stroke and compression ratio. Layout, firing order, rod geometry and sleeve valves cannot be reproduced, so declare them.
  2. Enter your cited values: bore, stroke, compression ratio, rows and cylinders per row, and supercharger and fuel-grade settings if they apply. Record the preset’s remaining defaults.
  3. If your cylinder count is beyond the sliders, simulate the largest supported count with the same bore, stroke and r, then scale only extensive outputs (power, torque, fuel flow) by nactual/nsim by hand. Intensive quantities (bmep, bsfc, ηb, imep, the P-V card) do not scale. The same applies if a slider cannot reach your bore or stroke: enter the closest allowed values, then compute your engine’s power and torque by hand from the simulator’s bmep using your actual Vd,total and RPM (P = bmep · Vd,total · N / nR). Your engine assignment never changes; only the way you model it does.
  4. Know which values are inputs. Volumetric eff. and Friction MEP are sliders you set, not measurements. Leave them at the preset default unless you have a cited value, and write them down. The propeller and reduction-gear section is a simulator feature: leave it at default and say so.
  5. Set your ambient temperature from your Parameter Card. Set Redline to the highest value the slider allows at or above your rated RPM so the sweep covers it.
  6. Fixed-RPM readings. For values at f3Nrated, first try reading the dyno curve. If it does not give exact values, hold full throttle and adjust Prop pitch / load until the live RPM settles at your target, then read HP, Brake Nm, Indicated Nm, BMEP, MAP, IAT, EGT and L/h from the live panel. State which method you used.
  7. Unit checks: verify the HP unit by comparing the displayed HP with 2πNτ/745.7 (state the result). Convert fuel flow from L/h to kg/h with ρf = 0.72 kg/L (aviation gasoline) or 0.745 kg/L (pump gasoline).
  8. Validate your proxy. Compare the (scaled) simulated peak power with the published rated power of your locked mark. Report the percent error and give two reasons for it. A large error is acceptable if it is explained.
  9. If the compression-ratio slider cannot reach your engine’s r (for example the slider stops at 8.0:1 and your engine is 6.5:1), enter the nearest allowed value rsim and correct the results by hand, to first order, at the same air and fuel flow:
    ηOtto(r) = 1 − 1/rkhot−1,   ρη = ηOtto(ractual)/ηOtto(rsim)
    imepsim = 4πτi/Vd,total (from the Indicated Nm readout),   imepcorr = ρη·imepsim
    bmepcorr = imepcorr − (imepsim − bmepsim),   &Wdot;b,corr = bmepcorr·Vd,total·N/nR,   bsfccorr = ṁf/&Wdot;b,corr
    Use the corrected values (and your actual r in Vc and ηOtto) in Sections 1 and 2, and say that the P-V card was generated at rsim, so its peak pressure is higher than your engine’s. The radial preset may allow a lower minimum than the other presets, so check each preset.

Page 2 of your report is a Preset-Mapping Declaration covering the preset used, nsim, every value you changed, every default you did not, and every real feature that could not be reproduced. An honest declaration earns credit; hiding a mismatch will earn a deduction.

Section 2: Performance Calculations (30)

  • Dyno peaks: power, torque, and the RPM of each (4).
  • bmep at peak power, computed both ways (nR&Wdot;b/Vd,totalN and 4πτ/Vd,total), with the two values compared with the simulator’s BMEP readout (6).
  • bsfc and ηb at f3Nrated, converting L/h to kg/h with your stated ρf, using the 3600/(bsfc·qc) form and stating qc (8).
  • Air-fuel ratio check at f3Nrated: record the Volumetric eff. slider; compute ṁair = evρiVd,totalN/2 with ρi = Pi/(RTi) from the MAP and IAT readouts; then AFR = ṁair/ṁf and compare with the simulator’s AFR readout (3).
  • ηOtto twice: with k = 1.4 and with your khot; state the gap to ηb in percentage points (6).
  • Units, significant figures, boxed answers (3).

Section 3: Bottleneck and Tuning Challenge (25)

  • Bottleneck analysis tied to your track and objective, with at least one cited source (6).
  • Tuning log, at least five runs. Run 1 changes your track variable by exactly s1; explain your reasoning before each later run; record power, torque, bsfc and peak cylinder pressure for every run (8).
  • Constraint check: tuned peak cylinder pressure must not exceed 1.30 × the stock peak (a proxy for knock and structural limits) (3).
  • Result: change in your objective as a percentage versus stock, with a one-paragraph engineering explanation (4).
  • Screenshot #2 with code and credentials visible (4).

Section 4: Indicator Diagram, Loss Attribution and Reflection (30)

  • Screenshot #3: Cylinder 1 P-V diagram with code and credentials visible (3).
  • imep by hand (6): read at least 12 (V, P) points, integrate with the trapezoid rule, imep = Wi/Vd. State whether the value is net (with pumping loop) or gross. Also compute imep = 4πτi/Vd,total from the Indicated Nm readout and compare the two values.
  • Compression-stroke analysis (6): pick two points on the compression curve; find the polytropic exponent n; compute W1–2 and, using the trapped mass m = P1V1/(RT1), the heat transfer Q1–2 from the first law. State the direction of heat flow and compare n with k.
  • Loss attribution (5): ηm = bmep/imep, fmep = imep − bmep, ηind = ηb/ηm, then ηb = ηOtto × (ηind/ηOtto) × ηm with your numbers; name the largest loss. Compare your hand fmep with the Friction MEP slider setting and explain the difference (pumping and other effects).
  • 250-word technical reflection (10), prompt chosen by engine:
    • Prompt A (engines 1–10): why was your layout superseded or limited? Address at least two real mechanisms (heat transfer through multi-bank jackets, friction from gearing and extra crankshafts, variable specific heats at high temperature, torsional vibration) and tie one claim to a number you computed.
    • Prompt B (engines 11–12): the layout survives only in niche products. Explain the trade-off against a conventional V or inline alternative and tie one claim to a number you computed.
    • Marked on specificity to your engine and numbers (5), correct physics (3), clarity (2).

8. Integrity and Verification

  1. Daily code: a verification code is posted on Canvas each day at 8:00 AM. Every screenshot shows the code for the day it was taken, handwritten and beside your monitor, plus your Student ID and Full Legal Name. Write the date on the slip. Pasted or unwatermarked screenshots receive zero.
  2. Handwritten math: all calculations are by hand (paper or stylus tablet). Typed math, equation editors and LaTeX are not accepted in calculation pages.
  3. Individual work: identical checkpoints, tuning logs or P-V reading points across submissions will be reviewed. Discussing concepts is allowed; sharing numbers is not.
  4. Oral check: a random sample, and any flagged submission, gets a 5-minute oral check on one handwritten calculation and one tuning decision from the student’s own report.
  5. Citations: specifications without a citation (title, page or table, year) lose credit.
