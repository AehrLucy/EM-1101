ME 3115 Midterm: Historical and Experimental Engine Forensics Lab

Internal Combustion Engines

1. Overview and Objective

In partial replacement of the scheduled midterm, you will analyze one historical or experimental four-stroke, spark-ignition reciprocating engine using the virtual engine bench at realenginesimulator.com. You will (1) set up and document the baseline, (2) compute geometry, kinematics and performance by hand, (3) run a constrained tuning study aimed at a bottleneck of the engine’s layout, and (4) use the engine’s own P-V indicator diagram to reconcile the ideal air-standard cycle with real loss mechanisms.

Several students share each engine, but your numbers will differ: your checkpoints, crank angle, tuning track, design objective, first step and hot-gas k are generated from your Student ID (Section 4). Matching numbers between two submissions trigger an integrity review. Estimated effort: 10 to 14 hours.

2. Scope and Formulas (Course Notation)

Every engine in the pool is a four-stroke SI piston engine, so the same formulas apply to all of them. Use the notation and constants of the course Formula Reference: bore b, stroke s, cylinders nc, nR = 2, specific-heat ratio k. Cold air-standard constants: R = 0.287 kJ/kg·K, cp = 1.005, cv = 0.718 kJ/kg·K, k = 1.4.

TopicFormulas used in this lab
GeometryVd = (π/4) b² s  |  Vd,total = nc·Vd  |  r = (Vc+Vd)/Vc = 1 + Vd/Vc, so Vc = Vd/(r−1)  |  ε = a/l = s/2l
KinematicsMean piston speed: Úp = 2Ns [m/s] (N in rev/s)
V(θ) = Vc[1 + ((r−1)/2)((1−cosθ) + (1/ε)(1 − √(1−ε²sin²θ)))]
Up(θ) = (π/2)Úp sinθ [1 + εcosθ/√(1−ε²sin²θ)]
Power & MEPBrake power: &Wdot;b = 2πτN  |  bmep = nR&Wdot;b/(Vd,total·N) = 4πτ/Vd,total
Indicated power: &Wdot;i = nc·Wi·(N/nR), with Wi = imep·Vd
ηm = &Wdot;b/&Wdot;i = bmep/imep  |  fmep = imep − bmep
Fuel & efficiencybsfc = ṁf/&Wdot;b  |  ηb = &Wdot;b/(ṁfqc) = 1/(bsfc·qc)  |  ηb = 3600/(bsfc[kg/kW·h] · qc[kJ/kg])
Breathingev = ṁin/(ρiVd,total(N/2)), ρi = Pi/(RTi) (only if the simulator reports air flow)
CyclesηOtto = 1 − 1/rk−1  |  isentropic: T2/T1 = rk−1, P2/P1 = rk
First law & polytropicQ1–2 − W1–2 = mcv(T2−T1)  |  P2/P1 = (V1/V2)n, T2/T1 = (V1/V2)n−1  |  W1–2 = (P1V1 − P2V2)/(n−1)

What differs between engines: forced induction raises bmep (formula unchanged; ηOtto still uses the geometric r); sleeve valves (Sabre, Centaurus) are modelled with poppet valves in the simulator, so declare it; fuel heating value qc is 43.5 MJ/kg for aviation gasoline engines and 44.0 MJ/kg for pump-gasoline engines, unless the simulator states otherwise. Diesel, Dual and Brayton cycles from the course are not used here because no engine in the pool is a diesel or two-stroke.

5. Simulator Approximation Declaration

Page 2 of your report states: (1) the cylinder count you simulated (nsim). If the simulator cannot handle your engine’s cylinder count, simulate the largest supported count with the same bore, stroke and r, then scale only extensive outputs (power, torque, fuel flow, air flow) by nactual/nsim by hand. Intensive quantities (bmep, bsfc, ηb, imep, P-V diagram) do not scale. (2) How you represented unsupported features (sleeve valves, supercharger or turbo staging, multiple crankshafts, cooling). (3) Any simulator default that differs from your cited specification. An honest declaration earns credit; hiding a mismatch costs credit.

7. Report Sections and Grading (100 points)

Section 1: Geometry, Kinematics and Baseline (15)

  • Screenshot #1 and completed Spec Table with citations (4).
  • By hand, with unit conversions: Vd, Vd,total, Vc; Úp at f1Nrated and at Nrated (6).
  • At your assigned θ, at Nrated: V(θ) and Up(θ) using the course slider-crank formulas (3).
  • Check that your Vd,total matches the published displacement within 1%, or explain why not (2).

Section 2: Performance Calculations (30)

  • Dyno peaks: power, torque, and the RPM of each (4).
  • bmep at peak power, computed both ways (nR&Wdot;b/Vd,totalN and 4πτ/Vd,total), with the two values compared (6).
  • bsfc and ηb at f3Nrated, using the 3600/(bsfc·qc) form and stating qc (8).
  • Volumetric efficiency at f3Nrated if the simulator reports air flow; otherwise a one-line explanation (3).
  • ηOtto twice: with k = 1.4 and with your khot; state the gap to ηb in percentage points (6).
  • Units, significant figures, boxed answers (3).

Section 3: Bottleneck and Tuning Challenge (25)

  • Bottleneck analysis tied to your track and objective, with at least one cited source (6).
  • Tuning log, at least five runs. Run 1 changes your track variable by exactly s1; explain your reasoning before each later run; record power, torque, bsfc and peak cylinder pressure for every run (8).
  • Constraint check: tuned peak cylinder pressure must not exceed 1.30 × the stock peak (a proxy for knock and structural limits) (3).
  • Result: change in your objective as a percentage versus stock, with a one-paragraph engineering explanation (4).
  • Screenshot #2 with code and credentials visible (4).

Section 4: Indicator Diagram, Loss Attribution and Reflection (30)

  • Screenshot #3: Cylinder 1 P-V diagram with code and credentials visible (3).
  • imep by hand (6): read at least 12 (V, P) points, integrate with the trapezoid rule, imep = Wi/Vd. State whether the value is net (with pumping loop) or gross.
  • Compression-stroke analysis (6): pick two points on the compression curve; find the polytropic exponent n; compute W1–2 and, using the trapped mass m = P1V1/(RT1), the heat transfer Q1–2 from the first law. State the direction of heat flow and compare n with k.
  • Loss attribution (5): ηm = bmep/imep, fmep = imep − bmep, ηind = ηb/ηm, then ηb = ηOtto × (ηind/ηOtto) × ηm with your numbers; name the largest loss.
  • 250-word technical reflection (10), prompt chosen by engine:
    • Prompt A (engines 1–10): why was your layout superseded or limited? Address at least two real mechanisms (heat transfer through multi-bank jackets, friction from gearing and extra crankshafts, variable specific heats at high temperature, torsional vibration) and tie one claim to a number you computed.
    • Prompt B (engines 11–12): the layout survives only in niche products. Explain the trade-off against a conventional V or inline alternative and tie one claim to a number you computed.
    • Marked on specificity to your engine and numbers (5), correct physics (3), clarity (2).

8. Integrity and Verification

  1. Daily code: a verification code is posted on Canvas each day at 8:00 AM. Every screenshot shows the code for the day it was taken, handwritten and beside your monitor, plus your Student ID and Full Legal Name. Write the date on the slip. Pasted or unwatermarked screenshots receive zero.
  2. Handwritten math: all calculations are by hand (paper or stylus tablet). Typed math, equation editors and LaTeX are not accepted in calculation pages.
  3. Individual work: identical checkpoints, tuning logs or P-V reading points across submissions will be reviewed. Discussing concepts is allowed, but not numbers:).
  4. Oral check: a random sample, and any flagged submission, gets a 5-minute oral check on one handwritten calculation and one tuning decision from the student’s own report.
  5. Citations: specifications without a citation (title, page or table, year) lose credit.
